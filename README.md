## Body

### What Are Pumpkin Seeds?
Pumpkin seeds are the edible seeds of pumpkins and squashes. They are nutritionally dense, providing zinc, magnesium, iron, healthy fats (omega-3 and omega-6), phytosterols, and antioxidants including vitamin E and polyphenols.

### Why Pumpkin Seeds May Help with PCOS
PCOS is linked to insulin resistance and hormonal imbalance. The nutrients in pumpkin seeds address both:

**Zinc:** Acts as a cofactor for enzymes involved in insulin synthesis, glucose metabolism, and steroid hormone regulation. Zinc supports the synthesis of follicle-stimulating and luteinizing hormones, which facilitate ovulation.

**Magnesium:** Contributes to insulin receptor signaling and oxidative stress reduction. A 2021 study of 1,000 women with PCOS published in Frontiers in Endocrinology found that lower serum magnesium was associated with higher insulin resistance and higher testosterone levels.

**Healthy fats:** Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids support cell membrane health and have anti-inflammatory effects relevant to PCOS.

### What the Research Says
A 2026 narrative review in PMC examined functional foods and nutraceutical compounds for PCOS. It found that pumpkin seeds, along with flaxseed, sunflower, and sesame seeds, showed promising effects when used as part of seed-cycling protocols. A 12-week clinical trial found women with PCOS receiving a daily mixed-seed supplement (15g of each seed powder per day) experienced improved hormonal profiles, insulin sensitivity, and ovarian morphology.

However, direct randomized controlled trials on pumpkin seeds alone are not yet published. The evidence is promising but preliminary.

### How to Include Pumpkin Seeds
- A small handful (15-30g) daily as a snack
- Added to salads, yogurt, or oatmeal
- Ground and mixed into smoothies
- In seed-cycling protocols: pumpkin seeds are typically recommended in the first half of the menstrual cycle

### Seed Cycling and PCOS
Seed cycling is a dietary strategy that rotates four seeds through the menstrual cycle: flax and pumpkin seeds in the first half, then sesame and sunflower seeds in the second half. A 2025 systematic review in Cureus evaluated 10 studies (n=635) and found seed cycling was associated with improved menstrual regularity, reduced PMS symptom severity, and favorable modulation of sex hormone levels "” though the evidence is based on small sample sizes.
